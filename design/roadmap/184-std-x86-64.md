# 184. Extend the `std` port (milestone 27) to x86_64

**Status: NOT-STARTED.** Minted 2026-08-28, calef, discussed directly: milestone 27 (`std` on the
native ABI) shipped **BUILT** for aarch64 and riscv64, and nothing tracks the third architecture's
gap as its own artifact. It currently lives as one clause inside milestone 161's own remaining-work
tally ("1 wants an `x86_64-unknown-nife` target and a `std` farm (milestone 27)") and as
`kernel/src/user/std_service.rs`'s `NO_STD_EXERCISER` constant ("there is no `x86_64-unknown-nife`
spec or farm yet"). This block gives it a home.

**Gate: NONE.** No design fork: the shape is already built twice, on the other two architectures,
and this is following an established pattern rather than inventing one.

## What this is, in brief

Milestone 27 built a custom rustc target per architecture (`aarch64-unknown-nife`,
`riscv64-unknown-nife`) whose `std` compiles and links directly against this kernel's capability
ABI (Hermit's shape, not a POSIX shim): allocation from the process's own untyped, `stdout`/`stderr`
over the console endpoint, `Instant`/`SystemTime` off the virtual counter, `std::fs`/`std::net`
backed by capability-granted servers or honestly `Unsupported`. `std_exerciser`
(`user/src/std_exerciser.rs`, or wherever the current program lives) proves it end to end on both
built architectures. Nothing equivalent exists for x86_64: no `x86_64-unknown-nife.json` target
spec, no `std` farm built against it, and `std_exerciser` itself skips there
(`std_service::NO_STD_EXERCISER`, 5 of x86_64's own skip count tonight).

## Why it is not urgent, and why it is not free either

**Not urgent**: nothing else in this tree currently blocks on it. Milestone 23's vendor-component
ambition (`std` widening "runs real workloads" to most of crates.io) is the reason milestone 27
existed at all, and that ambition is still served by two of three architectures having it; x86_64's
own kernel port (milestone 161) is itself still PARTIAL for reasons unrelated to `std`.

**Not free**: milestone 27's own text says "Effort: unpriced (it depends on another project's
toolchain and API, which the history here cannot bound)," and nothing checked here revises that for
x86_64 specifically. A third target spec, `-Zbuild-std` wired against it, and the PAL's own
architecture-specific pieces (the allocator's untyped-growth hook, the console-endpoint stdio
wiring, the virtual-counter binding) all need porting, checked against x86_64's own ABI rather than
assumed to be a mechanical copy of the other two.

## A real, worth-checking-first connection to milestone 164

Milestone 164 (x86_64 userspace can't build `aes`, therefore `fs_server`) already investigated a **separate**
`x86_64-unknown-nife.json` userspace target as one of its two candidate routes (Route 2: an x86
userspace target that keeps SSE, distinct from the kernel's own `x86_64-unknown-none`), specifically
because the kernel's own target has SSE disabled tree-wide and re-enabling it there would require
new, currently-unbuilt FPU/SSE context-switch state the scheduler does not carry. If 164 ends up
building that target for its own reason (SSE support for `aes`), **this milestone should reuse it
rather than mint a second, near-identical `x86_64-unknown-nife` target** — a `std` farm target does
not need SSE for its own sake, but sharing one target rather than carrying two subtly different
custom x86_64 userspace targets is the cheaper, less confusing shape. Whoever picks either
milestone up should check the other's status first.

## What it does not decide

Whether `std::fs`/`std::net` should be wired for x86_64 as part of this milestone or left
`Unsupported` until their own backing servers exist there — matching milestone 27's own original
phased build (phase one: allocation, stdio, time; phase two: fs/net once servers existed). The
honest default, absent a reason to do otherwise, is the same phasing: prove the PAL and
`std_exerciser` first, wire `fs`/`net` once `fs_server` itself works on x86_64 (which is milestone
164's own job, not this one's, if Route 1 there is taken instead of Route 2).

## Prior art

Milestone 27's own two built targets are the direct precedent and the thing to copy the shape of,
not re-derive: `targets/aarch64-unknown-nife.json` and its riscv64 twin, and `notes/std.md` for the
PAL's own design. Hermit (the closest external prior art, std's PAL over a non-POSIX unikernel ABI)
is already milestone 27's own citation and applies identically here.
