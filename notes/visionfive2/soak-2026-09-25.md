## The one sentence it licenses

**radon did 10.2 billion IPC round trips and 4.1 million cross-core thread handoffs over 8.16 hours
without the wake gate refusing a wake, without a wrong reply, and without a worker stalling.** That
is the sentence milestone 219 licenses and nothing more. It is a confidence about one arrangement of
one workload on one board, not a verdict on the concurrency.

## The draw, and why it was the fastest yet

The spawn census split every group. One drift event at the first beat (`drifted=11`) moved the
workers to the arrangement below, and `drifted=0` held for the remaining eight hours:

```
soak-test-census: core=1 threads=3 G0 G2 G3
soak-test-census: core=2 threads=8 R0 C0 C0 C0 R1 C1 C1 C1
soak-test-census: core=3 threads=7 G1 W2 R3 C3 C3 C3 W3
soak-test-census: core=4 threads=6 W0 W1 R2 C2 C2 C2
```

Three grinders share core 1, so two cores carry no grinder at all. The rate was 350,000 a second,
nearly double the best draw of 2026-09-03 (188,687 a second). That fits `notes/soak.md`'s grinder
co-location reading, where what starves a group is a grinder sharing its core. It is one more
supporting observation, and still not the slow-boot census that would test the prediction.

Every full hour added between 501,669 and 503,888 crossings. That flat rate is what milestone 245
(a soak cannot tell a flat run from a productive one) says this instrument cannot tell apart from a
run that stopped exploring. So "steady" means the machine kept doing the same work, not that it kept
finding new interleavings.

## Against QEMU, with the same tree

`script/soak-test --arch riscv64 --for 45s` on patagonia gave 16,264 round trips a second,
`wakerate` about 399, and 8,755 crossings in 40 s, with 0 / 0 / 0. radon ran the IPC workload about
21 times faster than the emulator and crossed cores at about two thirds of its rate. Neither run went
red, so the silicon-only question did not arise.

## Anomalies, each with its excerpt

1. The beat is 5.05 s, not 5. Of 5,817 intervals, 278 step `t` by 6 rather than 5, about one in
   twenty: `t=100s beat=20` is followed by `t=106s beat=21`. Each beat's 5 s is counted from the end
   of the previous beat's work (`watch` in `kernel/src/soak.rs`), so this is not a clock fault. Guest
   time agrees with the host: the console's 29,400 s watch ends at t=29,368 plus a boot. Read `t=`
   for a duration, never the beat count.
2. `deferred` rose from 0 to 3: the first `deferred=1` at t=15,381 s, then `=2` at 28,000 s and `=3`
   at 28,666 s. `sched::wakes_deferred` counts wakes parked because the target was still standing on
   a CPU, which its own doc comment calls ordinary under load.
3. The raw capture held 14 bytes outside printable ASCII, none inside the soak. They are U-Boot's
   autoboot backspaces and six bytes (`00 00 01 00 00 00`) between U-Boot's `clk u5_dw_i2c_clk_apb
   already disabled` and the kernel's banner. The `tr` in `notes/job-mix.md` step 5 stripped them
   before the commit.
4. `script/board-image --soak` printed `NOT SEALED` and exited 1, twice, for a pair that booted. A
   soak build never reaches measured boot, so its trust root is dropped at link time. This is
   `crates/sealed_pair`'s own recorded bug, owned by milestone 563 (a seal check that reads bytes
   cannot see a check that was dropped). It cost this session about half an hour.

## What it rules out, and what it does not

It makes one thing unlikely: a wake-protocol or IPC-rendezvous defect on this silicon, in this
arrangement, that fires more often than about once per 1.4 million cross-core handoffs. Zero events
in 4.1 million puts the 95% upper bound at three, which is the rule of three.

It says nothing about a rarer defect. Nor about one that needs an arrangement this boot did not
draw: a slow draw crosses about 275 times less often, 0.51 a second against 140. Nor about a path
the soak does not drive, which includes every device interrupt but the tick route's, page-fault
storms, and process creation under load.

And it is one boot. On the boots axis of the defect curve it is one point, and the
randomized-scheduler model `notes/soak.md` cites says a second boot is worth more than a ninth hour.
