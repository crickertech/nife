1. **design/roadmap/526-the-x86-tsc-calibration-takes-the-smallest-of-several-windows.md — "by the mean" should be "by the median," and as written the sentence is mathematically false.** The passage says: "The median is +0.00% at every count from three upward, which is why reporting the worst case rather than the *mean* was the instruction that mattered: by the mean, the defect was fixed at three windows and had never been very bad at one." The properties cited — "fixed at three windows" and "never very bad at one" — are properties of the **median** column (+0.00% from 3 up; +0.36% at 1). They cannot be properties of the mean: with a 99th percentile of +884% and a worst of +1153%, the top two boots alone put a floor of roughly +10% on the mean error at one window, and the +120%/+131% tail puts a floor above +1% on the mean at three windows. Worse, the sentence inverts the document's own lesson: the mean is the one common summary that would *not* have hidden a one-sided heavy tail (it is dragged by it); the median is the one that did hide it — which is exactly how the old `timer.rs` BUGS came to say "off by a per cent or so." In a record whose entire argument is "choose the cap by the tail, not by a central statistic," naming the wrong central statistic is a substantive error a reader checking the claim against the adjacent table will trip over.

2. **notes/benchmarks.md (the inserted CORRECTION block) — the claim that "the debug-versus-release argument and the 4.2x are ratios within a boot, where the calibration cancels exactly" cannot be true as stated.** The correction itself establishes, two sentences earlier, that the section rests on "eleven boots" that each "stored a different wrong rate," and the section's own context line says "Six boots debug, five release." A debug-versus-release comparison is therefore necessarily **across** boots, and a ratio of a debug-boot `ns/iter` to a release-boot `ns/iter` does not cancel the calibration — it inherits the quotient of two different wrong rates. The claim is only salvageable if the section's argument is specifically a comparison of two *within-boot* ratios (e.g., benchmark-to-benchmark in each build), but that is not what the sentence says. This matters because the correction is the authoritative statement of which published conclusions survive the defect; as written it over-assures, telling readers a cross-build conclusion is calibration-free when the document's own premises say otherwise.

3. **design/roadmap/526-...md — "the minimum is an unbiased [estimator]" contradicts the same section's correct framing.** The paragraph states every window "is an *upper bound* on the true rate" and that the minimum "converges on the truth from above," then calls the minimum "an unbiased one" in the next breath. An estimator that is always ≥ the truth, and strictly above it with positive probability, is biased upward; the accurate words are *consistent* and *one-sided*. The sentence's whole purpose is estimator justification ("An average would be a biased estimator for precisely the reason the minimum is an unbiased one"), so the mislabel is load-bearing, not decorative.
