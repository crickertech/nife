1. **notes/benchmarks.md** (the CORRECTION blockquote added at the 2026-08-24 table) — It declares safe "every conclusion here that rests on a ratio between two rows of the same run, which is all of them: the debug-versus-release argument and the 4.2x are ratios within a boot, where the calibration cancels exactly." That cannot be true: the section ran six debug boots and five release boots, so any debug-versus-release ratio pairs numbers from *two different boots*, and the notice itself states each of the eleven boots stored a *different* wrong rate. The calibration cancels only within a single boot; across builds the ratio is off by the ratio of the two boots' stored rates, which is unknown and unrecorded. This matters because the notice is the authoritative statement of what remains quotable, and it certifies as exact the one class of conclusion the defect actually does reach.

2. **notes/benchmarks.md** (same notice) — "Nothing can recover the true values: the rate each of these eleven boots stored was not written down." This contradicts the tree's own finding, restated in the new section and owned by notes/tsc-under-tcg.md, that under plain TCG on this host the TSC ticks at exactly 1000.000 MHz to within 42 ppm. The stored rate is not needed to recover the truth: the true ns/iter is the ticks column (which the notice itself says is unaffected) divided by 1e9. If the ticks are published, the table is recoverable to ~42 ppm; if they are not, the notice should not be reassuring readers that the ticks are "unaffected." As written it overstates the loss and gives the wrong reason for it, which will stop anyone from simply recomputing the table.

3. **design/roadmap/526-the-x86-tsc-calibration-takes-the-smallest-of-several-windows.md** — "An average would be a biased estimator for precisely the reason the minimum is an unbiased one." The minimum of several upper bounds is not unbiased: each window is exact or inflated, so the minimum is still biased upward for any finite window count — it is a *tighter upper bound* that "converges on the truth from above," as the same section and the deleted proposal both say correctly, and as the document's own BUGS ("a probabilistic filter, not a bound") concedes. In the design record for a measurement fix, the estimator claim is the sentence future readers will quote, and "unbiased" overclaims the guarantee the rest of the document is careful to qualify.
